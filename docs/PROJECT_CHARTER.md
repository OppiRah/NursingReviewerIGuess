# Project Charter — Software Product Discovery

## Mission

Determine whether an existing student-built application, a reusable subset of it, or a nearby problem should become a legitimate software product. The output is **a defensible decision**, not maximum idea count or a premature codebase.

## Dual goals

1. **Career/engineering:** Demonstrate architecture judgment, systems thinking, reliability, security, testing, documentation, deployment literacy and maintainability. The owner must understand and explain generated work.
2. **Commercial:** Serve a sufficiently painful, recurring, reachable problem with plausible willingness to adopt and pay. No unsupported TAM or revenue estimates.

These are different axes. A portfolio-valuable tool may not be a business; a practical B2B workflow may be technically modest. Score separately.

## Product formats allowed

SaaS, desktop, local-first, self-hosted, open-core, open source with optional hosted service, single-license utility, narrowly scoped B2B automation, developer tooling, educational tooling, or specialized Philippine-market software. **Do not force cloud multi-tenancy onto a local problem.**

## Existing candidate areas

A. Developer portfolio and engineering-evidence tooling.  
B. QA, regression and release-readiness workflow.  
C. BuildTrack-derived contractor inquiry/estimate/change-order workflow.  
D. ShortsLab-derived rights-aware stream clipping and review workflow.  
E. CALSS-derived academic code-similarity investigation workflow.  
F. Jairus-derived local voice/desktop automation.  
G. Notification Bridge-derived cross-device event/overlay tooling.

New adjacent ideas are allowed only when a clearly described customer problem and independent evidence justify them.

## Operating assumptions to verify

- Owner is a fourth-year CS student in the Philippines with limited time, budget and operational capacity. These are planning assumptions; obtain specific ceilings before committing to paid services.
- Familiar with Python, C++, TypeScript, Astro, Android/Kotlin, testing, desktop integration, browser automation and AI-assisted development. **Familiarity is not proof of production readiness.**
- Existing repos may be private, collaborative, incomplete, unsafe for publication or unsuitable for reuse. Inspect only with permission; verify licenses/rights before derivative commercialization.
- Claude = strategy synthesis + research/task delegation; Claude Code = authorized repo-level execution; optional local LLM = low-risk private retrieval/explanation; ChatGPT = optional counter-review.

## Non-negotiables

- Research **before** product selection; validation **before** substantial implementation.
- Cite original sources with access date, plan and geographic currency context; do not invent market gaps, demand, statistics, reviews, endorsements or pricing.
- Separate observation, interpretation, assumption, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Record competitors who *already solve* the alleged gap, including open-source and low-cost alternatives.
- Respect intellectual-property ownership, licenses, platform rules, FERPA/GDPR/Philippine Data Privacy Act applicability (do not assert legal coverage without jurisdiction-specific review), consent and data minimization.
- Never represent similarity as proof of student misconduct; do not build unauthorized copyrighted-content harvesting.
- No credentials, personal student records, real customer data, unlicensed media or private repo contents in shared notes.
- No commits, pushes, installations, external sign-ups, payments, deployments or third-party contact without explicit authorization for that action. For research documents, local edits are permitted once a scoped task is approved.
- No automatic transition from research to app implementation.

## Research questions

1. Who exactly experiences the recurring pain, with what trigger, frequency and measurable consequence?
2. Which alternatives do they currently use (including spreadsheets, email and doing nothing)?
3. What incumbent capability disproves our presumed advantage?
4. What behavior or money is committed to solving it today?
5. Can a solo developer ship and support a safe MVP while studying?
6. How will we reach users for interviews, pilots and distribution?
7. What is the cheapest falsifiable validation test?

## Explicit out of scope (until owner selects product)

Building backend/frontend, paid API setup, auth/billing, telemetry, mobile binaries, scraping at scale, social posting, automated emailing, deploying a website, public GitHub changes, buying a domain, sourcing customer data, choosing a framework for an unknown product.

## Definition of useful output

A source-linked landscape, gap hypotheses with counterevidence, a shortlist with explainable trade-offs, accessible customer validation, one decision record and an intentionally narrow potential MVP **only after owner sign-off**.
